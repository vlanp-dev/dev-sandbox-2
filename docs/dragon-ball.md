# Dragon Ball Franchise Snapshot

**As-of date:** 2026-10-30

This document is a short, general overview of the Dragon Ball media franchise.
It is a status snapshot rather than a complete history, and it is not intended
to be authoritative. Franchise details change over time, so specific claims
should be checked against current primary sources.

## Origins: the manga

Dragon Ball is a Japanese manga series written and illustrated by Akira
Toriyama. It was serialized in Shueisha's *Weekly Shōnen Jump* from 1984 to
1995 and was later collected into 42 tankōbon volumes. The story follows
Son Goku from childhood martial-arts adventures through increasingly
cosmic-scale battles, and it is generally divided into a younger-Goku era and
a later "Z" era beginning with his adulthood and Saiyan heritage.

The manga is the foundation of the franchise: most animated, film, and game
adaptations either adapt its arcs or extend its characters and setting.

## Anime

The franchise's animated presence began with the *Dragon Ball* anime, produced
by Toei Animation and broadcast in Japan from 1986 to 1989, adapting the
earlier portion of the manga. It was followed by *Dragon Ball Z* (1989–1996),
which adapted the remainder of the original manga run and became the most
widely recognized entry internationally.

Later animated works include *Dragon Ball GT* (1996–1997), an anime-original
sequel not based directly on Toriyama's manga, and *Dragon Ball Super*
(2015–2018), which continued the story after *Z* with Toriyama credited for
the original concept and story outlines. A manga version of *Super* has been
drawn by Toyotarou under Toriyama's supervision. More recently, the anime
series *Dragon Ball Daima* aired from late 2024 into 2025.

## Films and specials

Dragon Ball has a long run of theatrical films, beginning with anime-era
movies in the 1980s and 1990s and continuing with modern entries. Notable
later films include *Dragon Ball Z: Battle of Gods* (2013), *Dragon Ball Z:
Resurrection 'F'* (2015), *Dragon Ball Super: Broly* (2018), and *Dragon Ball
Super: Super Hero* (2022). Television specials and OVA-style releases have
also appeared over the years.

## Spin-offs and related works

The franchise has produced a number of spin-offs and side stories, including
the *Dragon Ball Heroes* promotional anime and arcade game line, various
manga side stories and one-shots, and Toriyama's related works such as *Jaco
the Galactic Patrolman*. Merchandise, collectibles, and theme-park and event
appearances form a substantial part of the franchise's footprint.

## Games

Dragon Ball is a long-running presence in video games, spanning arcade,
console, handheld, and mobile platforms. Well-known series and titles include
the *Budokai* and *Budokai Tenkaichi* lines, *Dragon Ball Xenoverse* and
*Xenoverse 2*, *Dragon Ball FighterZ*, *Dragon Ball Z: Kakarot*, and the
*Sparking!* series, including *Dragon Ball: Sparking! Zero*. Mobile titles and
arcade card games have also been long-running.

## Status notes

- The franchise spans manga, anime, film, games, and merchandise, but the
  exact current state of any line should be confirmed with up-to-date sources.
- Akira Toriyama, the franchise's creator, died in March 2024. Works released
  after his death should be checked for their specific credits and status.
- This snapshot deliberately avoids exhaustive lists of arcs, films, games,
  or release dates.
