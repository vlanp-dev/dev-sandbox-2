# Coffee Brewing Cheat Sheet

A quick reference for six common brewing methods. All ratios and brew times
below are rough, approximate starting points rather than authoritative
figures, and none of them are tested specifications. Treat them as a place to
begin, then adjust to taste.

## Pour-over

Hot water is poured over a bed of ground coffee in a filter, and the brew
drains through by gravity. The slow, controlled pour gives a clean cup with
good clarity.

- Approximate ratio: about 1:16 coffee to water.
- Approximate brew time: about 2.5 to 4 minutes.
- Tip: Pour in slow, steady circles so the coffee bed stays evenly saturated.

## French press

Coarse grounds steep fully in hot water before a metal mesh plunger separates
them from the liquid. The result is a fuller-bodied cup that keeps some fine
particles and oils.

- Approximate ratio: about 1:15 coffee to water.
- Approximate brew time: about 4 minutes of steeping.
- Tip: Press the plunger slowly and stop at the surface rather than forcing it down.

## Espresso

Pressurized hot water is pushed through a compact bed of fine grounds to make
a small, concentrated shot. It is usually served on its own or used as the base
of milk drinks.

- Approximate ratio: about 1:2 coffee to water in the cup.
- Approximate brew time: about 25 to 30 seconds.
- Tip: Keep the dose and grind consistent so one variable changes at a time.

## AeroPress

Coffee and water are combined in a chamber, then pushed through a filter by
hand. The method is quick, forgiving, and easy to clean.

- Approximate ratio: about 1:14 coffee to water.
- Approximate brew time: about 1.5 to 2.5 minutes.
- Tip: Rinse the filter first so it seats cleanly and does not add a papery taste.

## Moka pot

Steam pressure from a heated lower chamber pushes hot water up through a basket
of grounds. The strong, heavy-bodied result sits somewhere between drip coffee
and espresso.

- Approximate ratio: about 1:10 coffee to water.
- Approximate brew time: about 4 to 5 minutes on the heat.
- Tip: Take the pot off the heat as soon as the flow starts to sputter.

## Cold brew

Grounds steep in cool water for an extended period instead of being brewed
with heat. The slow extraction produces a smooth, low-acid concentrate.

- Approximate ratio: about 1:8 coffee to water for a concentrate.
- Approximate brew time: about 12 to 18 hours of steeping.
- Tip: Dilute the concentrate to taste before serving it over ice.
